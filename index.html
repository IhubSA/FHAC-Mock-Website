About the club

A strong family ethos,
and a mountain on
the doorstep

FHAC sits in the Fish Hoek valley. We back participation over victory at any cost — and from that, members have still gone on to represent the province and the country.

September 1977

How it started

Four people founded the club: DJ Price, Don Holgate, Paddy Mills and Bert Thompson. Getting registered took some creative problem-solving — there weren't enough runners in the valley to meet the requirement, so they found some.

By 1978 the club had launched the South-Easter Fun Run, the first fun run in the area. What began as a cross-country and short-distance club now covers every distance and discipline: road, trail, cross-country, walking and triathlon.

Our aim

“To promote athletics as a sport with a view to the optimal development of the athletics potential residing within our community.”

Club standards

Five tiers, four races,
one year

Run four of the eight recognised distances between 1 November and 31 October and you'll be awarded at the annual awards evening. The tier you receive is the lowest of your four qualifying performances — so the weakest run sets the award.

Only official ASA events and FHAC time trials count. parkrun and other clubs' time trials do not. Races run outside Western Province should be sent to the standards coordinator yourself.

Which event are you entering?

PUFfeR is not an open race. You must have finished an ultra of 50 km or more in the six months before race day, have real mountain running experience, attend the pre-race briefing, and nominate a volunteer for checkpoint duty.

Runner details

Your qualifying run

An ultra of 50 km or longer, finished within six months of race day. We verify it against the event's official results.

Matched. Two Oceans Ultra 2027, bib 11408, 05:41:22 — found in the official results. Nothing further needed.

Nominate your volunteer

Every entrant provides one person for checkpoint duty. Your entry isn't confirmed until they accept.

They'll get an SMS with a one-tap accept link. You can swap them out any time before the briefing.

Indemnity and race rules

1. Risk. The route crosses Table Mountain National Park and Silvermine over exposed mountain terrain, in weather that changes without warning. I enter at my own risk and accept full responsibility for my own safety.

2. Experience. I confirm I have substantial mountain running experience and can navigate the route in poor visibility.

3. Cut-offs. I will retire at any checkpoint where I miss the cut-off, and I will follow any instruction from a marshal, medic or the race director.

4. Mandatory kit. I will carry the full kit list — jacket, space blanket, charged phone, whistle, and 1 litre of water — and accept that I'll be stopped without it.

5. Environment. Zero tolerance on littering. Dropping anything on the route, including a gel sachet, means immediate disqualification and no future entry.

6. Briefing. Attendance at the pre-race briefing is compulsory. No briefing, no start.

Payment

Entry fees are not refundable, but your place can be transferred to another qualified runner up to seven days before the race from your entry page.

Substitutions

An injured entrant can hand their place to another qualified runner up to seven days before the race. The incoming runner re-enters their own licence, qualifier and indemnity — nothing is inherited.

Entry fees move with the place. The two runners settle payment between themselves; the club doesn't refund or re-charge.
